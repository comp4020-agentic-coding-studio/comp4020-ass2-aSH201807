## Source #14 — "从零开始写COC TRPG模组" (my own column notes)

Me / undated / personal column-style notes, 5 sections / local file

### Key idea

Five linked design moves, in the order a Keeper actually does them: (1) turn
a personal spark of interest into a premise using a small set of generic
Mythos "flavors" (ancient, unknown, chaos, space) rather than starting from
lore; (2) fuse that spark with an existing Mythos element into a concrete
truth/antagonist; (3) commit to a structure (linear vs. open) and a hook
(passive vs. active) before detailing scenes; (4) deliver that truth through
three clue types (literature/social/trace) split into two functions
(guiding vs. revealing); (5) gate access to clues with puzzles and skill
checks that are never allowed to hard-block the story, and staff it with
NPCs that exist for a stated reason.

### What I think

This is a practitioner's checklist, not a theory — it's opinionated and
sometimes contradicts "textbook" advice (e.g. it argues against difficulty
for its own sake, against padding NPC headcount, against ever letting a
failed roll delete the only path forward). That opinionated quality is what
makes it teachable: each section already comes with a rule stated as a
rule ("不要把检定变成卡关点"), a worked example, and a story of what goes
wrong when the rule is broken. It's also honest about being one person's
preference ("以我个人经验为准，如有冲突请以你自己的想法为准") rather than
claiming to be the only correct approach — that stance is worth keeping in
the course's own voice rather than smoothing it into neutral textbook prose.

### Implication for my course

This becomes the primary content spine for weeks 2–9 (secondary sources
below fill in weeks 1, 10 and cross-check rather than lead):

| Week | Title | Section of source #14 used |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | From Inspiration to Scenario Premise | 确定题材 / 寻找灵感 / 通用克苏鲁元素 |
| 3 | Truth, Threat and Antagonist | 把契合的融合起来 (the Dulce/Kunyan worked fusion example) |
| 4 | Scenario Structure & Investigation Flow | 构思模组流程 — time/place/hook, 开放性 vs 线性 |
| 5 | Locations, Scenes and Nodes | the tunnel → base → camp worked scene chain |
| 6 | Clue Architecture | 线索设置 — guiding/revealing × literature/social/trace, taught **alongside** Blase's core/supporting/advantage frame (source #5) as a second lens, not a replacement |
| 7 | Mysteries, Puzzles and Red Herrings | 谜题设置 — four puzzle types, difficulty gradient, don't design puzzles for their own sake, multi-threading |
| 8 | Skill Checks and Failure Design | 检定设置 — never gate progress on one roll, success/failure both need diegetic wording, SAN is a consequence not a goal |
| 9 | NPCs as Investigative Systems | NPC设置 — purpose-first, one vivid trait, don't let NPCs monologue at each other |

Weeks 1 (framing), 10 (atmosphere/escalation) and 11–12 (playtesting/
assembly) draw on the secondary sources (Blase, Petersen/Alexandrian,
Chaosium quickstart) plus the "汇总/预演/补完" closing section of this
source.

### Harness implication

Course content for weeks 2–9 should cite this source's rules directly
(in the course's own voice, since it's the author's own material — no
external citation needed), and use Blase et al. only where a rule benefits
from a second, more formally-argued perspective (clue taxonomy in week 6
being the deliberate case). No `spec/` check follows from this directly;
it's narrative/pedagogical content, not a mechanically-checkable fact.
