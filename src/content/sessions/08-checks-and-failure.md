The most common way a scenario breaks at the table isn't a bad clue — it's
a single failed roll deleting the only route forward. This studio is about
finding every check in your draft that could do that, and rewriting it so
a failure changes the cost or the framing rather than the outcome.

## Before the session

Go through your scene map and mark every point where you currently plan to
call for a roll.

## In the session

For each marked check, ask: if this fails, can the player still reach the
next scene, just more slowly, more expensively, or with less certainty?
Rewrite the failure text so it says what the character *does* notice or
do, never a flat "nothing happens." Sanity checks get the same treatment —
they're a consequence of what the character just witnessed, not a target
you're trying to hit.

## Afterwards

Read back through your whole draft as if you were the unluckiest possible
player. If any scene still has a hard stop behind one roll, that's this
week's fix before the studios move on to NPCs.
