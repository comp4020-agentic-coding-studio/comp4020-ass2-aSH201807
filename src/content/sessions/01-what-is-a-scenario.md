A Call of Cthulhu scenario is five linked decisions, made in order: turn a
spark of interest into a premise, fuse it with a Mythos truth and
antagonist, commit to a structure, build a clue web that survives a bad
die roll, and staff it with puzzles, checks and NPCs that all exist for a
reason. Nothing else in this course is Mythos trivia — it's practice at
making those five decisions well.

## Before the session

Bring one idea, however rough: a creature or entity you find genuinely
interesting, a news story or unsolved case that stuck with you, or an
object that unsettled you for no obvious reason. It doesn't need to be
Cthulhu-shaped yet — that's next week's work.

## In the session

A worked walkthrough of the pipeline end to end, using a scenario neither
of your teachers wrote. Then, in pairs: two minutes to pitch your raw idea,
one probing question back from your partner. The question is the point —
it's the first sign of whether the idea can carry other people's attention.

## Afterwards

Open a scenario notebook — one document, any format — and write down your
idea plus the question your partner asked. Every assessment this semester
builds on what goes in that notebook, starting with the Scenario Pitch.
