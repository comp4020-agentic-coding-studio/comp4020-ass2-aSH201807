Penrose's office smells of pipe smoke and damp paper. Tide tables cover
every wall, decades of them, and one drawer is stuck — forced, then
carefully closed again from the inside.

Behind the tide tables you find his private logbook, the handwriting
tightening into a scrawl over its last twenty pages. The final legible
entry: *"The clock isn't broken. Corwin's turned it back on purpose,
same as his father did in '58 — trying to buy the town another cycle
before the debt comes due again. I mean to stop him doing it quietly.
Whatever the town owes, we should have stopped paying it forty years
ago."*

Nothing else in the office explains what "the debt" is. But it's clear
Penrose knew, and that it led him straight to the clock tower.
