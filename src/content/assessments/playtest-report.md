## The brief

> Run a real slice of your scenario with a real player, then report what
> actually happened and what you're changing because of it.

A read-through cannot substitute for this — the whole point of Assessment
3 is a player making choices you didn't predict. If you don't have a
regular group, use the playtest-matching arrangement your tutor keeps;
pairing with another student's draft counts, and one player is enough for
a meaningful test.

## What you submit

A short report (roughly one to two pages): what you ran and with whom,
at least one stuck point, at least one pleasant surprise, and the specific
changes you're carrying into your Final Scenario. Honesty about what broke
is worth more here than a polished account that avoids admitting anything
did.
