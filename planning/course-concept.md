# Course concept

## Working title

*How to Write a Call of Cthulhu Scenario*

## Central idea

Not "how to play CoC" or "Cthulhu Mythos 101," but how a Keeper turns a
vague horror idea into a runnable investigation scenario — the design
process, not the setting. Everything in the course is a decision a scenario
author makes (premise → truth → structure → clues → puzzles/checks → NPCs
→ playtest → assembly), not lore to memorize.

## Why this is worth a semester (niche + depth)

Scenario writing for investigation-structured horror TRPGs is a design
discipline with its own recurring failure modes — clue chains that break
on one failed roll, puzzles that gate the story instead of serving it, NPC
casts that bloat without adding anything a player can act on — and its own
recurring fixes, which is what makes 12 weeks of depth possible rather than
a single workshop's worth of tips. The course draws its authority from the
author's own scenario-writing and Keeper practice (`research/subject/`
source #14), cross-checked against published Keeper-craft guidance
(Chaosium's own quickstart, Andrew Blase's CoC 7e design guides, and the
Petersen/Alexandrian horror-scenario framework) so students see a personal,
opinionated practice held up against the wider field rather than either
alone.

## What a student can do at the end that they couldn't at the start

By the end of the course, a student can take a single spark of interest —
a mythos creature, a news story, an object that unsettled them — and turn
it into a complete, playable CoC investigation scenario: a stated truth and
antagonist timeline, a deliberate structure (linear or open, chosen on
purpose), a clue web that survives a failed roll, puzzles and skill checks
that never hard-block progress, a small NPC cast each with a stated reason
to exist, and a revision informed by an actual playtest rather than guesswork.

## What this course is NOT

- Not a Cthulhu Mythos literature or lore course — mythos content is used
  only as raw material for scenario design, never studied for its own sake.
- Not a general CoC *rules* course (character creation, combat resolution,
  the full skill list) — rules are referenced only where they bear on
  scenario design (e.g. how a skill check should be worded).
- Not a creative-writing or prose-craft course — the deliverable is a
  playable, runnable scenario, not literary short fiction.
- Not a claim of universal "correct" scenario design — the course states
  its own practitioner's opinions as opinions and invites disagreement,
  rather than presenting one method as the only valid one.
