# Assessment design

| # | Assessment | Weight | Due (week) | What's submitted |
|---|------------|--------|------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Scenario Pitch | 15% | after week 3 | premise, inspiration, central mystery/truth, intended horror, player experience |
| 2 | Investigation Architecture | 25% | after week 9 | scenario truth, antagonist timeline, locations, NPC network, clue web, investigation paths |
| 3 | Playtest Report | 20% | after week 11 | write-up of an actual playtest run: where players got stuck, unused clues/NPCs, unnecessary checks, surprising emergent moments |
| 4 | Final Scenario | 40% | after week 12 | complete playable scenario + Keeper notes, clue map, NPC sheets, handouts, playtest revision report |

Total: 100%.

## Resolved: the playtest requirement (Assessment 3) stays real

Kept as an actual playtest, not a simulated/self-run substitute. A student
without a regular group can satisfy it with a short session run for even
one or two players (friends, another student, an online pick-up group) —
the point is real player behaviour diverging from the author's expectations,
which a solo read-through cannot produce. Week 11 teaches what to watch for
before this assessment is due.

## Assessment-to-week mapping

Each assessment closes out the design stage taught immediately before it
(see `curriculum.md`'s table for the exact week list):

- Assessment 1 needs weeks 2–3 (premise, truth, antagonist).
- Assessment 2 needs weeks 4–9 (structure, scenes, clues, puzzles, checks, NPCs).
- Assessment 3 needs week 11 (playtesting method) applied to the Assessment 2 draft.
- Assessment 4 needs week 12 (assembly) plus the Assessment 3 revisions.
