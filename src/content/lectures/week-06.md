There's more than one useful way to think about a clue, and this course
teaches two of them side by side rather than picking a winner. This
lecture introduces both before the studio applies them to your own draft.

## Outline

- function: does a clue guide players toward the next scene, or reveal
  what the truth actually means once they have it
- delivery: literature (documents, records), social (what an NPC says),
  or trace (physical evidence at a scene)
- a second lens: Blase's core clues (needed to progress), supporting
  clues (context and colour) and advantage clues (reward careful play
  without gating it)
- why redundancy — more than one path to a core clue — matters more than
  which taxonomy you use to think about it
- why the three delivery formats matter beyond flavour: literature clues
  are close to guaranteed once a character looks, social clues should
  never hard-lock to one NPC, and trace clues let a threat register
  without staging a direct encounter
