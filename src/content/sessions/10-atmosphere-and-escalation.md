Horror in an investigation scenario mostly comes from what's implied and
kept off-stage, not from a monster described in full. This studio pairs
that idea with pacing: a threat that can kill a player character with no
warning isn't tense, it's arbitrary — escalation only reads as escalation
if the players had a chance to notice it coming.

## Before the session

Find the single most "monster-forward" description in your draft — the one
that spells out what the creature looks like in the most detail.

## In the session

Rewrite it to describe effect rather than appearance: what the players
hear, smell, or half-see, and what their characters' bodies do in
response. Then check your antagonist's threat timeline from week 3: does
it give the players at least a few visible warnings before it's capable of
killing someone, or does the danger jump straight to lethal?

## Afterwards

Apply the same rewrite pass to any other scene that currently over-explains
the threat. This is also the last studio before playtesting — a scenario
that's still explaining everything on the page tends to explain everything
at the table too.
