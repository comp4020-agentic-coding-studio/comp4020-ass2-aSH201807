Before any scene gets detail, two decisions have to be made: how the
players get pulled in (something happens *to* them, or they choose to
investigate), and how the scenario is shaped (one throughline that's easy
to run and hard to derail, or several branching paths that need more prep
and more improvisation to hold together). Neither choice is more advanced
than the other — they suit different session lengths and different
groups.

## Before the session

Reread your Scenario Pitch's truth. Ask which structure it actually
suits: does the truth unfold whether or not the players intervene (which
tolerates an open structure), or does it need a specific order of
revelation to land (which wants linear)?

## In the session

Write the opening scene — the one that puts players in the story — and
commit to linear or open on paper. Trade with a partner: they should be
able to tell, from the opening scene alone, roughly how much freedom
they'd have as a player.

## Afterwards

Everything from here — scenes, clues, puzzles, checks, NPCs — gets built
against this commitment. Changing structure later means redoing most of
it, so this is the week to be sure.
