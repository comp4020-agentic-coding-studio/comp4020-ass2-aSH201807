# Curriculum — 12-week outline

Each week is one design decision in the order a Keeper actually makes it,
not a topic list — see `research/subject/notes.md` (source #14 mapping
table) for exactly which of the author's own notes feeds which week.

| Week | Title | What it covers | Builds on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What Is a CoC Scenario? | Course framing: scenario-as-design-problem, not lore or rules. What "truth → clues → structure" means as a pipeline. Sets up why weeks 2–9 exist. | — |
| 2 | From Inspiration to Scenario Premise | Picking a subject (CoC vs. a homebrew Mythos-adjacent setting); finding a personal spark; the four generic Mythos flavors (ancient, unknown, chaos, space) as a toolkit for turning a spark into a premise. | Week 1 |
| 3 | Truth, Threat and Antagonist | Fusing the premise with an existing Mythos element into a concrete antagonist and causal truth (worked example: a real-world "Dulce base" hook fused with the Mythos "K'n-yan" race). | Week 2 |
| 4 | Scenario Structure & Investigation Flow | Committing to time/place/hook before detailing scenes; passive vs. active player hooks; linear vs. open scenario structure and what each costs/buys. | Week 3 |
| 5 | Locations, Scenes and Nodes | Turning a structure into a concrete scene chain — which locations exist, what's discoverable in each, what changes if players linger. | Week 4 |
| 6 | Clue Architecture | Two clue taxonomies taught side by side: the author's own (guiding vs. revealing; literature/social/trace) and Blase's (core/supporting/advantage) — students compare rather than being handed one "correct" model. | Week 5 |
| 7 | Mysteries, Puzzles and Red Herrings | Four puzzle types; difficulty as a design choice, not a flex; puzzles must serve the clue they gate; multi-threaded puzzles as the real mechanism behind "open" scenarios. | Week 6 |
| 8 | Skill Checks and Failure Design | Never gate the story's one path behind a single roll; success/failure both need a diegetic reason; SAN loss as a consequence of events, not a goal in itself. | Weeks 6–7 |
| 9 | NPCs as Investigative Systems | Purpose-first NPC design (why does this NPC exist); one vivid trait beats a biography; avoiding NPC-NPC dialogue that sidelines players. | Weeks 6–8 |
| 10 | Horror, Atmosphere and Escalation | Atmosphere technique (implication over description, threats kept off-stage) and escalation pacing, cross-checked against the Petersen/Alexandrian horror framework. | Weeks 3, 9 |
| 11 | Playtesting and Revision | Running an actual playtest of a scenario-in-progress; what to watch for (stuck points, unused clues/NPCs, unnecessary checks); revising from playtest evidence, not guesswork. Feeds directly into Assessment 3. | Weeks 4–10 |
| 12 | Scenario Assembly & Keeper's Final Pass | Assembling the finished materials (scenario text, clue map, NPC sheets, handouts) into something another Keeper could actually run cold. Feeds Assessment 4. | Week 11 |

## Assessment mapping

See `assessments.md` for weights; in outline, Assessment 1 (Pitch) lands
after week 3, Assessment 2 (Investigation Architecture) after week 9,
Assessment 3 (Playtest Report) after week 11, Assessment 4 (Final Scenario)
after week 12.

## Spec compliance

12 weeks, each a single dated `sessions` entry with `meta.week` 1–12 —
matches `spec/assignment-2-spec.test.ts`'s "runs across twelve dated
teaching weeks" check.
