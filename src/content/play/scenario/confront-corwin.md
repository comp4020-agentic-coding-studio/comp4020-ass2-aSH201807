"There's no debt," Corwin says, evenly. "Clock's just old. Runs how it
runs."

It's a good lie, delivered without a flinch — except in his hands. They
are wrong: crusted white at the knuckles with dried salt, the way skin
goes after hours in seawater, and they will not stay still on the
workbench while he talks. He notices you noticing and puts them in his
pockets.

He won't say another word about Penrose, the *Nightjar*, or the clock's
true purpose. But he's not surprised you asked, and he's not surprised
you're still here.

The tide turns at midnight. You have until then.

**What do you do?**
