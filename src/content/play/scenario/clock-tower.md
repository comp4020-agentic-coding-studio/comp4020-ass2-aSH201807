The tower door is open. Old Corwin Vane is inside, oiling a mechanism
that hasn't needed oil in years, and he doesn't seem surprised to see
you.

Up close, the clock's two hands aren't the plain iron shapes they
looked like from the hill — each ends in a small, worn brass disc,
stamped with a mark you half-recognise from a maritime museum: a
harbour token, the kind old shipping crews once carried as a paid
passage. Someone built them directly into the mechanism, not as
decoration.

Corwin follows your eyes to the hands and says nothing, waiting to see
what you'll ask him.
