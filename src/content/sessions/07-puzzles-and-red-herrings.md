A hard puzzle isn't automatically a good one — a puzzle is good when
someone enjoys solving it and it was worth the table time it cost. That
means using puzzles sparingly, and only where the clue behind it matters
enough to justify slowing the group down.

## Before the session

From your tagged clue map, pick at most one or two clues that are both
important and interesting to gate behind a puzzle. Everything else stays a
skill check — that's not a lesser design choice, it's the right one for
most clues.

## In the session

Design the puzzle, then swap with a partner and have them attempt it with
no help from you. Afterwards, ask them what they thought the puzzle was
*for* — if they can't connect it to what it reveals, the puzzle is testing
the wrong thing.

## Afterwards

Revise based on what your partner got stuck on or missed entirely. A
puzzle that only you can solve isn't ready for a scenario that other
people will run.
